# Review summary (public, 0.1.0-candidate)

This records the internal review of the candidate: who looked, what they raised, and what was done. It is
AI-assisted internal review, not peer review or independent reproduction.

## What ran

1. **Panel (round 1).** Five Claude reviewer roles, editor, methodology (proof audit of every section), domain
   (literature, attribution and novelty), perspective (readability and significance) and devil's advocate, run as two
   combined passes. Two reviewers recomputed the main certificate independently in exact arithmetic (the trace bound
   tr(T^128) and the three certified coefficients).
2. **Clean replay by the lead.** All bundle scripts rerun from a fresh copy; outputs byte-identical to the recorded
   ones; the edge list regenerated byte for byte.
3. **One revision round** with extensions: an explicit finite answer to Beaton and Brown's question (certified average
   size and exclusion of every nearby index as a most frequent size), the slow-mixing consequence for single-site
   sampling, the odd-order case of the central-mode threshold with explicit thresholds, an explicit form of the degree
   criterion, and explicit many-peak instances.
4. **Verification re-review and proof audit** of the new and rewritten mathematics (Sections 3.4, 5, 6.6, 8 and 9 and
   the match between the theorem statements and their proofs), with independent recomputations: the average size at
   120-digit precision, brute-force enumeration of small instances, the Section 9 inequalities in exact rationals, and
   the Section 5 window bounds. It found one mechanical blocker in `check_extensions.py` (a float overflow in a print
   statement and a threshold set above the true value 88.97) and minor wording items; all were fixed and the script
   was rerun to a clean exit.

No second-model (Astra/Codex) adversarial review took place for this release.

## Sources

All nine cited works were obtained and read: Alikhani–Peng (the conjecture), Beaton–Brown (rising half, minimum degree
2 log2 n, the mode–average question), Burcroff–O'Brien, Galvin–Zhang, Omar, Zhang, Beaton–Schoonhoven, Du–Heilman–Panova
and Bhattacharyya–Kahn. Attributions were checked against the source texts: the rising half is Beaton–Brown
Proposition 2.4, the union bound in Section 8 is Omar's Proposition 9, and the two-family principle is credited to
Bhattacharyya and Kahn.

## Main objections and dispositions

| Objection | Disposition |
|---|---|
| Correctness | No error that breaks a theorem was found in round 1, in the re-review or in the proof audit. |
| "Sharp" or "optimal" overstated | Restricted to the position of the maximum (a central mode at ceil(n/2)); the paper says the lower-bound graphs may be unimodal and places the unimodality threshold between 1/2 and 1 times log2 n. |
| "Answers Beaton–Brown" overstated | Split into an explicit finite answer (Theorem 1.3(b), certified) and an asymptotic linear separation (Theorem 1.3(a)) whose constant, of order 1e-25, is stated plainly (Remark 6.1). |
| Debt to Bhattacharyya–Kahn | Credited in Sections 1.2 and 1.4, including their remark on many local maxima; the new element is the covering constraint of domination, exactly equal families and an exact certificate. |
| Attribution in Section 8 | Rising half credited to Beaton–Brown, union bound to Omar; the new ingredient is the bound on the one-vertex additions and the weighted degree criterion. |
| Effective thresholds | Explicit instances with four, eight and sixteen peaks checked exactly; explicit thresholds 2^20 (even n) and 2^40 (odd n) in Theorem 1.4(iii); explicit Corollary 8.2 for n >= 4. |
| An error term in Section 5 | A bound on one cell probability was misstated in round 1; corrected, with the true order of the residual. |
| Transfer identity untested beyond the smallest case | `check_extensions.py` now compares the transfer identity and the mean with direct enumeration for four parameter pairs; the text says which identity each script confirms. |
| Context and notation | New introduction (problem, what was known, results, idea, roadmap, notation); a purpose sentence opens every section; symbol clashes removed. |
| Checks could pass vacuously under optimisation | `assert` statements replaced by explicit checks that exit with status 1, also under `python -O`; negative controls confirm this. |

## Limits that remain

The open problems in Section 10 of the paper: the least minimum degree that forces unimodality (between 1/2 and 1 times
log2 n), the size of the smallest counterexample, trees and forests, an explicit constant in Theorem 1.3(a), and the
role of the maximum degree. The literature search is recorded in RESEARCH_GATES.json.
